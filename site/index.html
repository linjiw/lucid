When training gets easier,
is the robot getting better?

We measure two failures that favorable training scores can hide: a curriculum narrows its physics ranges while return rises, and a policy keeps completing rollouts while its motion quality deteriorates.

Measure the conditions the policy can handle—and the motion it actually executes.

Current ICRA manuscript: When Training Gets Easier: Range Collapse and Tracking Drift in Humanoid Robustness Training. This is a diagnostic study. It does not claim that adaptive curricula outperform fixed randomization.

Completed evidence

Two measurements expose two different losses.

Range collapse

Across twelve scored runs, terminal return and independently evaluated robustness have Spearman rank correlation −0.73. One collapsed run loses 14.19 success-AUC points against fixed randomization on the same seed.

Never-shrink passes the preregistered empirical tolerance rule. Its mean difference from fixed is +0.60 points (SD 2.25, three seeds). The one-sided 95% lower bound is −3.19 points: this does not establish equivalence or statistical noninferiority.

Tracking drift

With the hard-practice mixture fixed, unanchored continuation changes nominal global MPJPE from 127.93 to 225.87 mm while completion stays at 100%. A reference-action anchor limits the final increase to 4.33%.

R1 passes empirical retention checks at all five sampled checkpoints and improves hard-condition tracking qualification by 5.18 percentage points over origin. This is one origin, one motion and one continuation seed.

Archived development / future research

Recovery-aware curricula remain the longer-term goal.

The released-controller path-input pilot and all sixteen initial/final evaluation cells are complete. Mean nominal tracking errors improve on all four development motions. In the sideways push cell, final completion is 126/128 versus 128/128 initially; that failure remains part of the result. No recovery bands were calibrated, so these results do not establish return-to-task recovery.

Multi-motion protection, learned recovery feedback, four-arm curriculum comparisons and hardware testing are outside this submission’s evidence. They require separate validation. No further GPU sweep is scheduled for them under the current freeze.
