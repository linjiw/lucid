LUCID: Recovery-Aware Curricula for Quality-Preserving Humanoid Robustness. We study how a capable motion-tracking policy can learn to withstand harder conditions, recover after a disturbance, and continue the requested task without losing its original quality.

“Robustness should expand what the robot can execute faithfully—not merely what it can survive.”

This is the target paper direction. Current evidence supports a bounded retention result and identifies released SONIC as a capable shared-origin candidate. Recovery-feedback superiority and real-robot transfer remain research questions.

01 / Completed retention experiment

Preserving the original motion is possible in this setting

Continuing robustness training can preserve completion while changing the motion substantially. We compared three continuation recipes from the same origin. R1, the reference-policy anchor recipe, is the only arm within the empirical retention budgets at every sampled checkpoint.

The comparison covers nominal and original-envelope conditions at 250, 500, 1,000, 1,500 and 2,000 iterations. Each global/local error mean must remain within +10% of origin, with completion falling by at most two percentage points. These are frozen development budgets, not physical safety limits or simultaneous confidence guarantees.

02 / Earlier local-origin screen · completed September 7 at 01:57 UTC

The longer motions expose a capability gap in the origin

The original four-second clip often ends before a recovery window can be observed. We therefore screened longer development motions before collecting recovery labels. The candidates were chosen from metadata and duration before seeing outcomes: curved walking, sideways walking and neutral stooping, plus the original motion as a control.

Frozen design

4 motions × 2 checkpoints × 128 aliases = 1,024 evaluation episodes. Nominal conditions; shared evaluation seed 8730; R1 fixed at its preselected 2,000-iteration endpoint.

Independent questions

Can the origin execute this motion faithfully? Does R1 retain its quality? Absolute task qualification and origin-relative retention are both reported.

Measurement boundary

The recorder captures translation, heading and articulation separately. This nominal screen has no disturbance-recovery endpoint. Zero-increment interval events are not pushes.

Inspect each motion

Decision: establish the shared multi-motion origin first. Curved and sideways walking often survive but fail tracking qualification; stooping never completes. Relative retention can pass even when both policies fail the task. These candidates cannot yet support a claim that LUCID preserves a set of previously solved motions.

R1 also increases curved-walking global error by 49.45%, failing that motion’s relative retention budget. All eight outcomes remain reported. We will not turn a favorable relative change on an unsolved motion into a capability-preservation claim.

The original control retains quality in this new seed: +8.79% global error, −0.97% local error, and 100% completion for both policies. Its +8.79% differs from the earlier +4.33% because the evaluation seed and panel size differ. It does not replace the earlier frozen endpoint.

What the recorder checks established

A separate eight-cell recorder experiment passed all four revised execution/retention parity comparisons and captured 380 aligned nonzero velocity-increment events. The first, stricter all-metric comparison failed because contact telemetry differed. Off/off repeats also showed contact-input differences; the underlying simulator cause is unresolved.

The revised protocol was frozen before new runs. It requires exact native execution audits and non-contact metrics while retaining five excluded contact-derived diagnostics in the report. The original gate remains failed. This validates execution measurement under that protocol; it does not validate physical safety, recovery thresholds or feedback efficacy.

03 / Target method · hypotheses to test

Understand recovery. Protect quality. Allocate practice.

Temporary deviation may be a useful recovery step. Persistent displacement, heading error or motion deformation is a failure to return to the task. LUCID’s proposed loop must distinguish those cases and use the distinction to improve training decisions.

01 → Observe recovery

Reference, command, execution

Combine motion intent and phase with strictly causal command and observation histories to estimate future recovery and persistent-mismatch risk.

Must outperform current errors, derivatives, simple history statistics and a strong predictor with the same history inputs.

02 → Preserve capability

Anchor what already works

Maintain retained-condition practice and reference behavior on states the origin handles well. Allow new recovery behavior where the origin cannot succeed.

The strongest fixed DR and curriculum baselines receive the same preservation mechanism.

03 → Allocate practice

Expand, hold, or gather evidence

Accept harder conditions only when observed capability and retained quality pass conservative checks. Give candidates a bounded practice budget before admission.

Unresolved decisions trigger bounded additional probes with prespecified sequential error control.

Retained skills + current frontier + limited candidate practice → updated policy → measured recovery and retention → next practice allocation

The objective

Increase tracking-and-recovery-qualified execution on a predeclared evaluation distribution, subject to per-motion quality and completion constraints. A larger randomization range or better survival alone is insufficient.

The decisive comparison

At a common total resource budget, compare full feedback with strong fixed allocation and a development-frozen schedule. Count probes, anchor collection and updates, observer training, and simulator transitions.

This is the proposed training-side framework. A learned recovery observer and a demonstrated closed-loop curriculum contribution are not completed results. The SONIC actor/critic interface remains the common controller foundation. The utility estimator and residual allocator remain gated.

04 / Evidence-led roadmap

What must be true before the full paper claim is warranted?

  1. Validate the common controller Candidate established

    Released SONIC solves the four development candidates. The common path-input extension passes same-state live action checks. A protected input-learning pilot is complete; tighter per-motion competence and recovery criteria remain to be calibrated.

  2. Protect the retained motion set Pilot evaluated

    A balanced four-motion nominal buffer now protects the input-learning pilot. Evaluate actual retention before extending protection to the original dynamics envelope. Replicate learning seeds, then independently trained origins; distinguish those two kinds of replication.

  3. Calibrate recovery with observable windows Pending

    Use separate origin calibration and validation episodes. Freeze translation, heading and articulation bands, phase handling, dwell and recovery horizon. Isolated event timing must leave enough uninterrupted observation; reset and reference-resampling frames cannot manufacture recovery.

  4. Test whether history adds useful information Pending

    Split full episodes, motions and origins before forming windows. Match instantaneous error and phase across different recovery outcomes. Measure held-out discrimination, calibration and lead time; then test whether the signal improves decisions.

  5. Compare the complete training loop Pending

    Use common preservation, controller and resource contracts. Include fixed practice, a frozen schedule, quality-aware and history-based curricula, and LUCID. The fixed/feedback × unanchored/anchored comparison separates preservation from scheduling.

  6. Verify recovery beyond the training scripts Pending

    Test unseen event phases, directions, repeated disturbances, correlated delays and combinations; then update independent-simulator comparisons. Hardware trials must measure nominal retention and actual return to task across short motions and long sequences.

If all benefits come from anchoring, we will attribute them to anchoring.

Scope and reproducibility notes
  • SONIC / Isaac Lab carries the research claims. BeyondMimic is a separate sandbox; its outcomes are not pooled into these results.
  • Global translation, heading, root-relative pose and heading-aligned articulation are measured separately. Scalar MPJPE components do not add to global MPJPE.
  • Tracking-qualified execution uses broad development limits. Recovery-qualified execution still requires calibrated event outcomes; physical safety is not certified.
  • Training origin and motion are statistical grouping factors. Hundreds of evaluation aliases do not become hundreds of independent trained-policy samples.
  • Shared-GPU wall times do not establish an efficiency ranking. Extra preservation, measurement and feedback computation must be counted.
