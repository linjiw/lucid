# Beyond Mismatch: Counterfactual Practice Utility for Sim-to-Real Humanoid Motion Tracking

## Abstract

Domain-randomization curricula for humanoid motion tracking typically react to current failure, tracking error, or command–execution mismatch. These signals indicate whether a motion–physics context is presently difficult, but they do not directly measure whether practicing that context will improve future deployment performance.

We propose **Counterfactual Practice Utility**, a measurement and curriculum framework that estimates the marginal value of training on a motion–perturbation context. Starting from the same policy checkpoint, we construct paired branch-and-continue interventions. A control branch continues training under the current LUCID distribution, while an intervention branch receives an equal-compute, localized increase in the probability of a candidate context. Their difference on a frozen, quality-qualified deployment suite defines the context’s multi-horizon practice utility.

Sparse utility measurements are used to audit common curriculum proxies, including latent command–execution mismatch, failure rate, tracking error, learning progress, advantage magnitude, and temporal-difference error. A lightweight estimator then predicts practice utility from motion, perturbation, contact, and policy-state features. Rather than replacing LUCID, the estimator produces a bounded residual reweighting around LUCID’s stable curriculum distribution. The residual has an exact identity point: when its coefficient is zero or predicted utilities are equal, the method recovers the original LUCID curriculum.

The central claim is deliberately falsifiable. If latent mismatch already predicts long-horizon utility, the learned residual curriculum is unnecessary. If it predicts only short-horizon improvement, the result separates optimization acceleration from asymptotic robustness. We evaluate on unseen motion families, held-out perturbation compositions, cross-simulator transfer, and long-horizon Unitree G1 deployment using tracking, smoothness, contact, energy, and actuator-quality constraints in addition to success.

The project asks a simple question: **not which experiences are hard, but which practice experiences make a humanoid better.**

## 1. Motivation

A robust humanoid tracker must learn from a heterogeneous space of training contexts:

[
x=(m,\phi,c),
]

where (m) denotes a motion clip or temporal segment, (\phi) is a structured vector of dynamics and sensing parameters, and (c) describes the contact regime.

Existing curricula generally use a present-time proxy:

[
s(x;\theta)
\in
{
\text{failure rate},
\text{tracking error},
\text{latent mismatch},
\text{TD error},
\text{learning progress}
}.
]

These quantities describe the current interaction between policy (\theta) and context (x). They do not directly describe the causal effect of allocating additional training to (x).

For a small policy update generated by context (x),

[
\theta'=\theta+\eta g_x,
]

the corresponding change in a deployment objective (J_Q) is approximately

[
J_Q(\theta')-J_Q(\theta)
\approx
\eta\nabla_\theta J_Q(\theta)^\top g_x
+
\frac{\eta^2}{2}g_x^\top H_Qg_x.
]

Difficulty proxies may correlate with the magnitude of (g_x), but improvement depends on its alignment with the deployment gradient, its variance, and the local curvature. A context can therefore be difficult but useless, easy but transferable, or locally beneficial but globally harmful.

## 2. Central Hypothesis

The proposal tests the following hypothesis:

> Current difficulty and command–execution mismatch are incomplete proxies for the marginal value of practice. Directly estimating multi-horizon, quality-qualified practice utility will reveal systematic difficulty–utility reversals and enable more effective allocation of humanoid training experience.

Three concrete predictions follow.

First, latent mismatch will be more predictive of short-horizon stabilization than of long-horizon deployment gain.

Second, contexts with similar mismatch will exhibit different utility depending on motion family, contact structure, perturbation type, and policy-training stage.

Third, a bounded residual utility curriculum will improve lower-tail robustness and held-out perturbation compositions without sacrificing LUCID’s training stability.

## 3. Base Curriculum

Let (\rho_k(x)) denote the training-context distribution produced by LUCID at curriculum epoch (k). Its domain-randomization support is controlled by the LUCID scalar (\lambda_k):

[
\rho_k(x)
=========

p(m),
p(\phi\mid\lambda_k),
p(c\mid m,\phi).
]

The proposed method does not initially replace this distribution. LUCID remains the safety envelope and default curriculum.

This choice provides a clear identity path and avoids requiring a second learned teacher to discover a safe curriculum from scratch.

## 4. Counterfactual Practice Utility

### 4.1 Localized intervention

For a candidate context (x), define a local kernel (\kappa_x) covering a small neighborhood of similar motion segments and perturbations.

We construct an intervention distribution:

[
\rho_{k,x}^{\epsilon}
=====================

(1-\epsilon)\rho_k+\epsilon\kappa_x,
]

where (\epsilon) is a small, fixed increase in training mass.

This intervention measures the marginal effect of practicing (x) while retaining most of the original curriculum.

### 4.2 Paired branch-and-continue experiment

From the same policy checkpoint (\theta_k), create two branches:

[
\theta_{k,H}^{0}
================

\operatorname{Train}_H
(\theta_k;\rho_k,\xi),
]

[
\theta_{k,H}^{x}
================

\operatorname{Train}*H
(\theta_k;\rho*{k,x}^{\epsilon},\xi).
]

Both branches receive identical model architecture, optimizer settings, PPO updates, environment interactions, and random-number streams (\xi) wherever feasible.

The counterfactual practice utility is

[
U_H(x\mid\theta_k)
==================

\frac{
J_Q(\theta_{k,H}^{x})
---------------------

J_Q(\theta_{k,H}^{0})
}{
\epsilon H
}.
]

Comparing against a continued-training control branch is essential. Comparing the intervention branch against the original checkpoint would confound context utility with the generic benefit of receiving additional PPO updates.

### 4.3 Multiple horizons

We measure utility at several nested continuation horizons:

[
H\in{5,20,100}.
]

A single intervention branch can be evaluated at intermediate checkpoints, reducing compute.

The resulting utility vector

[
\mathbf U(x)
============

[U_5(x),U_{20}(x),U_{100}(x)]
]

distinguishes immediate optimization gain from persistent capability improvement.

A context with positive (U_5) but negative (U_{100}) is an important curriculum failure case: it appears useful under a short proxy but produces long-term negative transfer.

## 5. Quality-Qualified Deployment Objective

Success alone is insufficient because a policy may remain upright while increasing oscillation, slip, impact, energy use, or actuator saturation.

We define a frozen development deployment suite (\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dev}}) and a pre-registered objective

[
J_Q(\theta)
===========

\mathbb E_{\tau\sim\theta,\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dev}}}
\left[
S_{\mathrm{track}}(\tau)
,
\mathbf 1
\left{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{no fall},\
\text{jerk}\le \tau_j,\
\text{foot slip}\le \tau_s,\
\text{contact impulse}\le \tau_c,\
\text{torque saturation}\le \tau_\tau
\end{array}
\right}
\right].
]

Thresholds are fixed before the main comparison using nominal simulation and hardware-safety considerations. They are not retuned separately for each method.

The final test suite (\mathcal D_{\mathrm{test}}), including held-out motions, held-out perturbation compositions, and hardware trials, is never used to label utility or train the estimator.

## 6. Proxy Audit

Before developing a new curriculum, we test whether one is necessary.

For every probed context, we record:

* LUCID latent-gap statistics, including median, p90, temporal slope, and variance;
* episodic return and failure probability;
* MPJPE and MPKPE;
* action jerk and oscillation spectral energy;
* advantage and value-loss statistics;
* absolute learning progress;
* motion embedding, phase, and contact regime;
* perturbation identity and intensity;
* current LUCID scale and policy-training stage.

We then evaluate how well each proxy predicts the sign and ranking of (U_H).

The decisive first figure is a **mismatch–utility phase portrait**:

* x-axis: current p90 latent mismatch;
* y-axis: paired (U_{100});
* color: perturbation family;
* marker: motion family;
* arrows: movement from (U_5) to (U_{100}).

If mismatch and long-horizon utility form a stable monotonic relationship, the proposal’s main premise is rejected and LUCID’s simple signal is strengthened.

If contexts with similar mismatch have opposite utility, the plot establishes that mismatch is a state descriptor rather than a sufficient curriculum value.

## 7. Utility Estimation

The oracle is intentionally sparse and expensive. It is used to generate diagnostic labels at selected policy checkpoints, not at every training epoch.

A lightweight estimator predicts

[
\hat U_\psi(x,h_k),
]

where (h_k) summarizes current policy state and recent curriculum statistics.

The first models should be deliberately simple:

1. linear and monotonic regression;
2. gradient-boosted trees;
3. a small MLP over motion, physics, and policy-state features.

A larger temporal model is introduced only if the simple estimators fail for identifiable representational reasons.

A first-order gradient-alignment estimator is included as a mechanistic baseline:

[
\hat U_{\mathrm{align}}(x)
==========================

\eta
\hat g_Q^\top g_x
-----------------

\beta|g_x|^2.
]

This tests whether explicit branch-and-continue labels provide information beyond local gradient alignment.

## 8. Identity-Preserving Residual Curriculum

The learned estimator does not output an absolute curriculum. It only reweights LUCID’s base distribution:

[
\tilde q_k(x)
=============

\frac{
\rho_k(x)
\exp(\hat U_\psi(x,h_k)/\tau)
}{
Z_k
}.
]

The deployed training distribution is

[
q_k(x)
======

(1-\alpha_k)\rho_k(x)
+
\alpha_k\tilde q_k(x),
]

with a small, bounded (\alpha_k).

This construction has an exact identity point:

[
\alpha_k=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
\hat U_\psi(x,h_k)=\text{constant}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
q_k=\rho_k.
]

Coverage floors prevent motion or perturbation starvation. A KL constraint limits distribution shift:

[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_k|\rho_k)\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{KL}}.
]

LUCID continues to control the global DR envelope. The residual curriculum initially changes only where training probability is allocated inside that envelope.

A per-factor residual to the DR bounds is tested later as an ablation, not introduced as a mandatory component of the main method.

## 9. Experimental Program

### Study 0: Killer Diagnostic

Select early-, middle-, and late-training LUCID checkpoints.

At each checkpoint, probe a stratified set of motion–physics contexts spanning:

* easy, intermediate, and difficult motion segments;
* latency, actuator, inertia, contact, sensing, and push perturbations;
* isolated factors and selected factor pairs;
* low, medium, and frontier intensities.

Run paired continuations and construct the mismatch–utility phase portrait.

This study is the gate for the rest of the project.

### Study 1: Difficulty–Utility Audit

Compare the ranking and calibration of:

* latent mismatch;
* failure probability;
* tracking error;
* learning progress;
* TD/value loss;
* advantage magnitude;
* gradient alignment;
* learned utility.

Evaluation includes sign accuracy, Spearman correlation, pairwise ranking accuracy, calibration error, and stability across continuation horizons.

### Study 2: Equal-Compute Curriculum Comparison

Train policies under identical environment-interaction and optimization budgets using:

* tuned fixed-wide DR;
* expert schedule;
* ADR;
* LUCID;
* failure-based hard-segment sampling;
* error-based motion sampling;
* learning-progress sampling;
* PLR-style value-loss prioritization;
* LUCID plus residual practice utility.

Report both learning-curve AUC and sufficiently trained final performance.

A method that improves only AUC is described as an acceleration method, not as having superior asymptotic robustness.

### Study 3: Structured Generalization

Hold out entire combinations during training, such as:

* high latency × low friction;
* payload shift × motor-strength mismatch;
* observation delay × external push;
* altered contact compliance × dynamic motion;
* joint-gain mismatch × long-horizon trajectory.

Evaluation distinguishes interpolation, single-factor extrapolation, and compositional extrapolation.

### Study 4: Motion Generalization

Split data by distinct trajectory and motion family rather than rendered clip count.

Training and test sets must not contain near-duplicate retargetings or the same executed trajectory under different visual scenes.

Report mean performance, lower-tail performance, and family-level confidence intervals.

### Study 5: Cross-Simulator and Hardware Evaluation

Train in Isaac Lab and evaluate frozen checkpoints in a second physics engine.

The final Unitree G1 study uses a larger set of motions and repeated trials per motion, with confidence intervals for binary success and hierarchical reporting across motion families.

Hardware data are used only for final evaluation, not for utility-label generation.

## 10. Falsification Criteria

The proposal is rejected or substantially simplified under any of the following outcomes.

### Falsification 1: Mismatch is sufficient

If latent gap reliably predicts the sign and ranking of (U_{100}) across motions, perturbations, and policy stages, a learned utility estimator is unnecessary.

The appropriate result is a stronger validation of LUCID and possibly a simple calibrated mapping from mismatch to curriculum action.

### Falsification 2: Utility labels are not identifiable

If paired branch variance remains larger than between-context utility differences despite common seeds and adequate replication, direct context utility is not measurable at the chosen granularity.

The project must either move to coarser context groups or abandon context-level claims.

### Falsification 3: Gains are proxy-only

If residual utility improves success but worsens smoothness, slip, contact impulse, energy, torque saturation, or hardware reliability, the method has optimized the wrong objective.

### Falsification 4: Acceleration without final improvement

If fixed-wide DR catches up under sufficient compute, the result is reported as sample-efficiency improvement rather than stronger final robustness.

## 11. Expected Contributions

The strongest possible outcome would contribute:

1. A causal measurement showing that current difficulty and latent mismatch are not sufficient indicators of long-horizon training value in humanoid control.

2. A paired branch-and-continue protocol for estimating the marginal utility of motion–physics practice contexts.

3. An identity-preserving residual curriculum that retains LUCID as a stable safety envelope.

4. A quality-qualified evaluation protocol separating survival from physically desirable execution.

5. A future-test benchmark covering unseen motion families, unseen perturbation compositions, cross-simulator transfer, and long-horizon hardware deployment.

Even if the learned curriculum does not outperform LUCID, the proxy audit remains valuable if it clearly establishes when mismatch, failure, learning progress, or TD error do and do not predict useful practice.

## 12. Practical Execution Plan

### Phase A: Instrumentation

Add context identifiers and independent physical-quality metrics to the existing LUCID training and evaluation pipeline. Freeze development and final test suites before running the utility study.

### Phase B: Oracle Pilot

Use three LUCID checkpoints, approximately 24–32 stratified contexts per checkpoint, and paired continuations with shared seeds. Evaluate nested horizons from the same branches.

The goal is not yet to train a curriculum. It is to determine whether the difficulty–utility mismatch exists.

### Phase C: Minimal Estimator

Compare simple proxy mappings, gradient alignment, tree-based models, and a small MLP. Select the smallest model that provides stable out-of-checkpoint utility ranking.

### Phase D: Residual Curriculum

Integrate the estimator as a low-coefficient residual over LUCID. Conduct identity-path, coverage-floor, and KL-radius ablations.

### Phase E: Full Evaluation

Run equal-compute simulation studies, held-out composition tests, cross-simulator evaluation, and the final Unitree G1 deployment.

## Thesis Statement

Existing humanoid curricula regulate training using how difficult a context is now; deployment performance depends on how practicing that context changes the policy later. We reformulate domain-randomization curriculum learning as counterfactual practice-utility estimation and learn only a bounded residual around a stable LUCID baseline.
